## Task 1: Unusual Uses Task
- Definition: This task challenges individuals in their ability to think of as many unusual and diverse uses as possible for a common object within a limited time frame. The object in question is typically everyday and familiar, such as a brick, paper clip, or newspaper.
- Example: Unusual Uses Task. You will be presented with a common object, and your task is to suggest as many unusual, innovative, or non-traditional uses for each object as you can think of. Let's think step by step. Please list unusual uses of  carpet

| Dimension       | What to Look For                                                         | 1                                    | 3                                  | 5                                                                                                  |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Fluency**     | Number of distinct, relevant uses                                        | 0–1 uses                             | 5–8 uses                           | ≥13 uses                                                                                           |
| **Flexibility** | Variety of use types (e.g., functional, aesthetic, social, metaphorical) | one category only                    | 3–4 categories                     | ≥6 categories across domains                                                                       |
| **Originality** | How rare or surprising the uses are                                      | all everyday (doorstop, paperweight) | some mildly odd (weapon, art base) | many unexpected yet plausible (brick as heat-battery, data artifact, seed for AI training analogy) |
| **Elaboration** | Detail in describing each use                                            | label only                           | short phrase (“build mini table”)  | rich mechanism (“crush brick to powder for natural pigment that filters light”)                    |


## Task 2: Consequences Task
- Definition: This task focuses on the ability to foresee consequences or outcomes of an unusual or hypothetical situation. For example, what would be the implications if animals could talk?
- Example: What would happen if the world's deserts suddenly turned into forests?Let's think step by step.

| Dimension       | What to Look For                                                                   | 1                              | 3                                    | 5                                                                                                              |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Fluency**     | Number of consequences                                                             | 1–2                            | 6–8                                  | ≥12 distinct effects                                                                                           |
| **Flexibility** | Diversity of domains (social, ecological, economic, legal, technological, moral …) | 1–2 domains                    | 3–4                                  | ≥6 domains                                                                                                     |
| **Originality** | Novelty of consequences                                                            | generic (“laws against noise”) | some less-common links               | multiple specific and unexpected but plausible chains (“AI translation obsolete because parrots become spies”) |
| **Elaboration** | Causal depth and mechanism                                                         | statement only                 | “because→therefore” chain or example | layered reasoning with second-order effects or quantitative hint                                               |

## Task 3: Just Suppose Task
- Definition: This task encourages imaginative and speculative thinking by asking participants to consider hypothetical, often fantastical, scenarios and their implications. For example, just suppose you woke up one morning and found you could fly. What would you do? List as many things as you can think of.
- Example: Just suppose you could decide the length of a day. How long would it be and why? Let's think step by step.

| Dimension       | What to Look For                                              | 1                       | 3                     | 5                                                                                                    |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Fluency**     | Number of actions/events imagined                             | 0–1                     | 4–6                   | ≥10                                                                                                  |
| **Flexibility** | Range of contexts (personal, societal, scientific, emotional) | one theme               | 3–4 themes            | ≥6 themes with clear shifts                                                                          |
| **Originality** | Imaginative twists                                            | typical (“fly to work”) | some inventive angles | multiple novel scenes or world-building ideas (“use flight to map micro-climates for urban gardens”) |
| **Elaboration** | Narrative detail and sensory depth                            | short list              | coherent mini-story   | vivid imagery + emotion + cohesive arc                                                               |

## Task 4: Situation Task
- Definition: This task is designed to assess creative thinking by evaluating how individuals respond to and interpret a given situation. This task emphasizes understanding social dynamics, empathy, and the ability to consider multiple perspectives or solutions. For example, if all books were to disappear, how would you gain knowledge?
- Example: If there were no more night-time, how would you sleep? Let's think step by step.


| Dimension       | What to Look For                                                | 1                        | 3                                         | 5                                                                            |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Fluency**     | Number of solutions/actions                                     | 1–2                      | 5–7                                       | ≥10                                                                          |
| **Flexibility** | Range of approaches (tech, social, educational, emotional etc.) | one approach             | 3–4 approaches                            | ≥6 approaches                                                                |
| **Originality** | Novelty of solutions                                            | obvious (“use internet”) | some non-obvious ideas (“oral libraries”) | highly original multi-layered solutions (“genetic memory backups in plants”) |
| **Elaboration** | Depth of explanation and feasibility                            | labels                   | one-sentence reasoning                    | detailed plan with impacts, actors, trade-offs                               |


## Task 5: Common Problem Task
- Definition: This task focuses on everyday problems that are familiar to most people, requiring participants to generate innovative and effective solutions. For example, organizing a cross-country road trip or building a tree house.
- Example: Common Problems Task. In this task, you will be presented with a scenario or situation. Your job is to think about it and identify as many potential problems or issues that may arise in connection with each situation. Let's think step by step. The scenario is: Overcoming language barriers in a multicultural workplace.


| Dimension       | What to Look For                                                         | 1                    | 3                                    | 5                                                                                           |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Fluency**     | Number of distinct problems                                              | 0–2                  | 6–8                                  | ≥13                                                                                         |
| **Flexibility** | Number of problem types (tech, env, human, logistic, legal, financial …) | 1 type               | 3–4 types                            | ≥6 types                                                                                    |
| **Originality** | Rarity of problems listed                                                | generic (“pressure”) | some specific (“biological hazards”) | multiple rare, cross-domain risks (“cyber-spoofing of acoustic comms,” “EEZ law conflicts”) |
| **Elaboration** | Mechanistic and causal depth                                             | labels only          | short mechanism                      | multi-step causal chains + examples + mitigation                                            |

## Task 6: Improvement Task
- Definition: Improvement. This task focuses on assessing an individual′s ability to enhance or modify existing objects or ideas. The given object is similar to the unusual uses task.
- Example: Creativity Improvement Task. You'll be presented with a object, and your task is to suggest as many ways as you can think of to improve the object. Let's think step by step. Here's the object: Shopping Cart


| Dimension       | What to Look For                                                                        | 1                | 3                                         | 5                                                                                         |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Fluency**     | Number of improvement ideas                                                             | 1–2              | 5–7                                       | ≥12                                                                                       |
| **Flexibility** | Variety of improvement domains (material, ergonomic, aesthetic, systemic, ecological …) | 1–2              | 3–4                                       | ≥6                                                                                        |
| **Originality** | Novelty of improvements                                                                 | incremental only | some creative features (“dual-loop clip”) | radical re-conceptualizations (“self-heating shape-memory clip that welds itself closed”) |
| **Elaboration** | Detail and function mechanism                                                           | names only       | basic “how it works”                      | technical mechanisms + materials + user context + trade-offs                              |


## Task 7: Imaginative Stories
- Definition: maginative stories. This task is designed to assess creativity through narrative and storytelling with a given prompt. This task emphasizes the ability to construct original, coherent, and imaginative stories, showcasing an individual′s creative potential in terms of narrative ability. Examples of given prompts are “The Invisible Elephant” or “The Book that Wrote Itself”.
- Example: You are to construct a narrative or story based on the prompt provided below. The story length are suggested around 500 words. Let's think step by step. The prompt is: The Ladder That Reached the Moon


| Dimension       | What to Look For                                           | 1                         | 3                                         | 5                                                                                                        |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Fluency**     | Number of distinct story elements (images, events, scenes) | ≤2                        | 4–6                                       | ≥10 developed beats                                                                                      |
| **Flexibility** | Variety of narrative moves (time, POV, genre, tone)        | single straight narrative | 3–4 devices (time shift, flashback etc.)  | ≥6 integrated devices / modes                                                                            |
| **Originality** | Novelty of concept and imagery                             | conventional plot         | one or two fresh images/twists            | multiple surprising yet coherent concepts and symbols (e.g., ladder as memory, moon descending to Earth) |
| **Elaboration** | Descriptive detail, coherence, arc                         | sketchy or incoherent     | clear beginning–middle–end + some imagery | rich description + emotional arc + symbolic resolution or ambiguity                                      |
